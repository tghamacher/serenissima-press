    in

    Closer spacing fits more music on each page, so a piece can stay on one opening without a smaller staff. In the closer settings the notes sit only as far apart as their words need, the way the Alamire workshop's scribes wrote. Printed books gave each note the full width of its syllable.

    Gothic is a rounded blackletter, used in the earliest music prints and in many Spanish and German books. It comes with long s and the old printers' ligatures. Roman type arrived later with the humanist presses.

    The printers worked on rag paper, made sheet by sheet on a wire mould; the choirbooks were mostly written on vellum, calfskin scraped thin. Aged ink turns the scribes’ iron-gall ink brown and the printers’ a warm black, a little uneven from note to note; on screen its edges are roughened too, as type pressed into paper, but not in print, where that would turn every page into a large picture. Turn off printing for clean rehearsal copies.

    Print the voice names

    Scribes ruled their pages before copying the music, so leftover staves still have lines. Printed books left that space blank.

    When a page fills up, its lowest voice continues at the bottom of the facing page. A matching sign at the break and where the music picks up shows the singer where to look.

    Printed music books ended with the printer’s mark and a line naming the city, the printer and the year. In partbooks each voice’s book ends with it, at the foot of the last page if there’s room.

    4Notation
    5Initials

    As in the Alamire workshop’s choirbooks: the Tenor’s initial is a T and the Bassus’s a B, with the rest of the name written beside it, and the words begin in full on the staff. The first voice keeps the text’s initial.

    The 1592 woodcut set only has A, D, F, G, L, N, O, Q and T; any other letter comes from the foliated woodcut. Rossi’s cadels have no K or W, so Franck’s are used for those. Every other alphabet has all 26 letters (J and U are written as I and V, as in the period books). The roman alphabets suit printed books, and the gothic ones suit manuscripts and early prints.
